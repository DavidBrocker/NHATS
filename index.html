NHATS: Social Isolation, Loneliness, and Cognitive Decline

A longitudinal analysis of NHATS Rounds 11–14

Authors

David Brocker

Maria Anderson

Published

November 23, 2026

Overview

The National Health and Aging Trends Study (NHATS) is a nationally representative, longitudinal study of Medicare beneficiaries aged 65 and older in the United States, with annual in-person interviews beginning in 2011. Each round re-interviews the same sample persons (SP), supporting analysis of change over time.

This project examines how loneliness and social isolation relate to cognitive functioning and cognitive decline in later life. Loneliness items were introduced in Round 11, so the analytic window spans Rounds 11–14, treating loneliness and isolation as time-varying predictors of concurrent and subsequent cognition.

Research aims

  1. Describe the distribution of loneliness and social isolation across Rounds 11–14.
  2. Model loneliness and social isolation as predictors of cognitive performance (immediate and delayed word recall).
  3. Test whether loneliness/isolation predict cognitive decline — within-person change in cognition across rounds.

Choice of social isolation measure

There is no single consensus metric for social isolation in NHATS. Recent work notes directly that inconsistent measurement limits comparability across studies (Yang and Jiang 2024). Two main families appear in the literature.

1. Structural / behavioral composite (Cornwell and Waite 2009; Cudjoe et al. 2020). Living alone, social-network size, and social participation. This is the measure coded in datapeak.R and used by He et al. (2026), who describe it as follows:

“Social isolation was measured using a four-domain composite index: living alone (household size = 1), no confidant (social network size = 0), restricted social participation (≥ 2 of 4 activities not performed: visiting friends/family, attending religious services, participating in clubs/classes, going out for enjoyment), yielding a 0–4 score with ≥ 2 indicating social isolation.”

— He et al. (2026)

2. Berkman–Syme Social Network Index (Berkman and Syme 1979), validated for NHATS by Pohl et al. (2017), who scored it as:

“Indicator scores were summed to create the social isolation score (no = 1 point, yes = 0 points; range = 0 to 6), with higher score indicating greater isolation.”

— Pohl et al. (2017)

Yang and Jiang (2024) adapt the Pohl measure to a 0–4 form with a different scoring direction (0 = severe isolation; 2–4 = socially connected), again illustrating the lack of a settled metric.

Recommendation. Use the structural four-domain composite as the primary measure — it matches the code and He et al. (2026) — citing Cornwell and Waite (2009) and Cudjoe et al. (2020), with Berkman and Syme (1979) as the conceptual root. To answer the “best metric” question defensibly, run a sensitivity analysis with an alternative operationalization (continuous 0–4 vs. dichotomous ≥ 2, and/or the Pohl et al. (2017) 0–6 measure) to show results are not an artifact of one measurement choice. Keep loneliness separate from isolation (perceived vs. structural); He et al. (2026) model them as distinct constructs.

Key constructs & operationalization

Variable names are from the Round 14 SP file; the 14 prefix changes per round (11–13). Question wording is verbatim from the NHATS Round 14 instrument (NHATS 2025); response codes are from the R14 SP Frequencies codebook.

Loneliness

wb14offelche5 is item (e) of the WB1 well-being battery (cheerful, bored, full of life, upset, lonely), introduced in Round 11:

“SHOW CARD WB1. During the last month, how often did you feel … lonely? Would you say every day, most days, some days, rarely, or never?”

— NHATS (2025)

Variable Response codes
wb14offelche5 (WB1e) 1 = Every day (7 days/wk); 2 = Most days (5–6/wk); 3 = Some days (2–4/wk); 4 = Rarely (once a week or less); 5 = Never. Missing: −9/−8/−7/−1

He et al. (2026) classify responses of “every day” or “most days” (codes 1–2) as lonely. Note a slight wording difference: the instrument reads “how often did you feel,” whereas He et al. quote “how often have you felt.”

Social isolation — four-domain composite (0–4; ≥ 2 = isolated)

Lives alone — hh14dhshldnum = 1 (derived household size).

No confidant — sn14dnumsn = 0. Derived from the SN name generator:

“The next questions are about who you talk to about important things in your life. This may include good or bad things that happen to you, problems you are having, or important concerns you may have.”

— NHATS (2025)

Restricted participation — ≥ 2 of the 4 items below answered No (each: 1 = Yes, 2 = No; −1/−7/−8/−9 missing):

Variable Verbatim question (NHATS 2025)
pa14vistfrfam (PA1) “In the last month, did you ever visit in person with friends or family not living with you, either at your home or theirs?”
pa14attrelser (PA5) “In the last month, did you ever attend religious services? This includes attending in person or online.”
pa14clbmtgrac (PA9) “In the last month, {besides religious services,} did you ever participate in clubs, classes, or other organized activities? This includes attending in person or online.”
pa14outfrenjy (PA13) “In the last month, {besides participating in club or group activities,} did you ever go out for enjoyment? This includes things like going out to dinner, a movie, to gamble, or to hear music or see a play.”

Candidate fourth flag — fl14noonetalk = 1 (SP has no one to talk to). See the reconciliation TODO above.

Cognition

Delayed-recall prompt (cg14dwrddlyrc), from the 10-word list task:

“A little while ago, I read you a list of 10 words and you repeated the ones you could remember. Please tell me any of the words that you remember now.”

— NHATS (2025)

Variable NHATS item (R14) Responses
cg14dwrdimmrc Derived — score, immediate word recall (CG8) 0–10 (−1 Inapplicable; −2/−3 proxy; −7 refused)
cg14dwrddlyrc Derived — score, delayed word recall (CG15) 0–10 (−1 Inapplicable; −2/−3 proxy; −7 refused)

Call-out: cg14prewrdrcl is CG7PRE — “intro to word recall”, a gateway flag (1 = continue, 2 = refuses), not a score. Use cg14dwrdimmrc (immediate) + cg14dwrddlyrc (delayed).

Modeling strategy

Principle. Choose each term by its causal role (confounder, mediator, or outcome), decided before looking at results — not by whether it improves fit. Covariate blocks are entered in a pre-specified order to show how the estimate changes; terms are never dropped after the fact.

Two senses of “hierarchical.” (1) Multilevel: Rounds 11–14 are repeated measures nested within persons, so a mixed model reflects the data structure. (2) Blocked entry: acceptable only when the blocks are theory-driven and declared in advance.

Primary model

Linear mixed model, outcome = word recall (immediate, then delayed), random intercept and slope by person:

  • Lagged exposure: isolation/loneliness at round t − 1 predicting cognition at round t, to reduce reverse causation (decline → withdrawal).
  • Within/between decomposition: person-mean (between-person level) plus deviation from own mean (within-person change) for each exposure.
  • Pre-specified blocks:
Model Terms
M1 Isolation only (+ time)
M2 Loneliness only (+ time)
M3 Isolation + loneliness
M4 M3 + demographics (age, sex, race/ethnicity, education)
M5 M4 + health/function (chronic conditions, depressive symptoms, hearing/vision, ADLs) — sensitivity; may be mediators

Dementia

Dementia is on the causal pathway (isolation → decline → dementia), so it is not entered as a covariate. Plan:

  1. Primary: exclude probable dementia at the first included round (“decline among people without dementia”). Confirm the classification variable in the codebook. Because the classification includes a memory domain, flag possible circularity with the recall outcome and run a sensitivity analysis.
  2. Separate aim: incident dementia as a time-to-event outcome (discrete-time or Cox).
  3. Effect modification: compare with/without dementia or test an interaction rather than adjusting.

Other issues

  • Proxy respondents have no recall scores (−2/−3) and no self-reported loneliness — describe this selection.
  • Attrition and death are informative — plan inverse-probability weighting or multiple imputation as sensitivity analyses.
  • Survey weights do not combine simply with mixed models — decide between population inference and within-cohort association.
